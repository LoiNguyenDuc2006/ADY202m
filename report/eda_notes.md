1. Hourly seasonality is the strongest cyclical pattern (strength=0.291), about 6.3x stronger than day-of-week (0.046) and 2.6x stronger than month (0.114) -- traffic is driven mainly by the daily cycle, not weekday/weekend or yearly season (Figure EDA 3).
2. Cross-country variation is substantial: the largest-to-smallest unit mean ratio is 1.85, exceeding the month-to-month variation (1.2x, RQ1a) -- the unit (country) carries more predictive signal than calendar season (Figure EDA 4).
3. cloud_inc shows no statistically significant correlation with traffic (Spearman rho=-0.001, p=0.361, n=526,060), independently confirmed by two separate pipelines (custom SQL and this standardised template) -- this is a genuine data property, not a computation error (Figure EDA 5).
4. Cross-correlation across lags finds the strongest coefficient at only rho=0.013 at a 630-hour (~26-day) lag with no plausible physical mechanism, reinforcing that cloud_inc has no meaningful lagged effect on traffic either (Figure EDA 6).
5. ACF at the 168-hour (weekly) lag is 0.875, well above the 0.7 threshold for a strong seasonal-naive baseline -- this justifies using t_lag168 as the Naive forecast and explains why RQ2 models must clear a high bar to add real value (Figure EDA 8). No regime shift was detected (largest month-to-month change = 0.163, below the 0.5 flag threshold).
